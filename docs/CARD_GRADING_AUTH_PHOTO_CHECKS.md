# Card grading scenarios, paid-niche authorization and local photo checks
Date: 2026-10-30. This is an incremental extension of the existing Expo/OnSpace Cloud application, not a production certification.

## Implemented
### Card Flipper calculator
Open Card Flipper home → **Raw versus graded**, or Specialist Evidence & Buying Review → **Compare raw versus graded sale scenarios**. Route `/card-grading` is registered as a Card-only dedicated route in the existing build registry; other niche APK route generation excludes it.

All numbers are entered by the user, including raw selling price, purchase cost, percentage/fixed sale fee, raw/graded sale postage, packaging, grading fee (tax/surcharges included), insured outbound and return shipping, days held, monthly holding expense, annual capital cost, three editable grade/return scenario labels, their sale values and probabilities. Numeric blanks are not silently zeroed. Probabilities must sum to 100%; they are not normalised. A lower/ungraded scenario is included in the initial form without invented probabilities or prices.

Outputs: raw net profit, probability-weighted graded net profit, grading uplift, probability of beating raw, possible net outcomes, full grading/shipping/holding costs, gross break-even and gross price matching raw profit. Zero-probability outcomes are excluded from the downside range. The formula assumes no buyer-paid postage; selling fees apply to sale price. Monthly holding uses 30 days and annual capital uses 365 days on purchase + grading + round-trip shipping. Returned/ungraded outcomes still pay sunk grading costs. These are user scenarios, not professional grading, authenticated values, sold-price evidence, predictions or a promise of profit. No new AI or paid data service is used. Calculator state stays in the current screen; no inventory record is overwritten.

### Shared paid-niche guard
`supabase/functions/_shared/paidNicheAuthorization.ts` is the authoritative guard used by `swish-identify` and `swish-islamic-identify`. It validates authenticated tenant membership before premium/cache/Stripe checks. Existing server-managed professional/business/enterprise/lifetime tiers retain their established tenant-wide semantics, including lifetime during a Stripe outage. Other users require verified paid access for the requested registered niche. These paid specialist endpoints no longer allow the old five-call free fallback; the old quota code remains unreachable behind the mandatory guard.

Stripe authorization requires server-owned customer `metadata.user_id` equal to the authenticated account. Email equality alone is insufficient. Conflicting subscription user metadata, incorrect product, unknown niche, cancelled/past-due/unpaid status and expired periods cannot grant access. Active/trialing subscriptions use the established product, item-level Stripe periods and subscription niche metadata. Legacy subscriptions without niche metadata grant Coins only, not every niche. Unbound legacy customers need a trusted ownership review; they are not automatically linked by email.

The new `swish-check-subscription` uses the same verified evidence logic for all ten niches and hydrates a server-only database cache. Cached paid grants last at most five minutes (and never beyond the subscription period). Cancellation is applied on successful refresh; an advisory lock and timestamp prevent older racing snapshots restoring revoked access. An Edge guard re-reads reconciled cache before authorizing. No webhook added. Account foreground and four-minute polling refresh billing only, not AI. Inventory waits for billing hydration and reloads afterwards to avoid a blank first fetch.

Installed additive migration: `20261030_paid_niche_authorization.sql`. Tables `niche_entitlements` and `niche_entitlement_checks` reference existing `public.user_profiles`, have RLS, and deny client/anonymous cache writes; the snapshot RPC is service-role only. Existing ownership policies remain and are ANDed with restrictive paid checks for authenticated item SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE. Listing SELECT/INSERT/UPDATE and valuation SELECT depend on access to the underlying item. Direct item APIs and the existing SECURITY INVOKER inventory view inherit those boundaries. Scoped inventory loaders surface authorization denial instead of apparently empty stock. Direct tenant insertion is revoked; existing provisioning still creates starter tenants. Client tier edits and historical specialist changes are blocked. Existing specialist identities, records, subscriptions and lifetime tiers were not rewritten.

Islamic re-identification verifies the target item belongs to the tenant and coin registry before privileged updates, retains its stored specialist ID, and merges attributes instead of dropping existing specifics. Remaining marketplace/import/admin Edge Functions have not been comprehensively refactored to use this guard: their own privileged authorization review remains a separate requirement. Do not describe this change as enforcement of every marketplace API. Public item-image URLs remain public by the pre-existing storage design.

### On-device blur/lighting measurements
`photoQualityService.ts` performs Expo thumbnailing to 256px-wide JPEG then typed `jpeg-js` decoding and deterministic grayscale analysis. No server AI quality call. On native, photo bytes/measurement stay on device; on Expo web they are processed locally by the same service. Existing remote stored photos may need downloading for a requested preflight; no new external image upload happens for these checks.

Measurements use the centre 70% region: mean luminance (0–255), dark/clipped fractions and variance of the four-neighbour Laplacian. Default advisory thresholds: mean below 45 or dark fraction above 55%, mean above 225 or clipped fraction above 55%, edge variance below 70. These are heuristics, not device-calibrated blur classification; dark/plain subjects, background, image noise, resizing and glare can mislead. No object segmentation or professional image-quality certification is claimed.

Camera/gallery selection records measurements on each captured image; shared regular and Islamic identification services run preflight before sending the AI request. Local immutable URIs deduplicate checks in a bounded 16-entry in-memory cache. Images process sequentially with an event-loop yield. Native decoding failures are explicitly unavailable, not successful quality checks. Per-photo metrics and targeted retake guidance appear in `/capture`; identification with warnings requires explicit review/override there. Retaking resets the override. Older dedicated scanners show the preflight error and can retake or use the shared review workflow. Stored older drafts without metrics are rechecked on identification; they are not falsely marked measured. No quality or AI calls are scheduled on ordinary navigation.
